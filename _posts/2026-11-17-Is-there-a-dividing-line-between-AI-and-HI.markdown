There are two points I already know I want to make in this post: (a) we have no idea how the human brain works, especially in terms of learning, and (b) if we understood this, we would have implemented this in AI, and humanity would be toast.

Are these claims true? And what would be the implications if they were or were not? But first, context. 

I studied psychology some time ago hoping to understand how the human mind works. As it turns out, psychology is not the best field if you want to understand anything. While psychologists have made some important observations and clever experiments concerning human behaviour, there is no strong theoretical framework connecting these dots. What's the connection between attachment theory and Pavlovian conditioning? Computer science, on the other hand, has multiple rigorous theoretical frameworks relevant to cognition that are deeply interconnected. Reinforcement learning, information theory, game theory, to name the most important off the top of my head. Of course, you will hear something about these ideas when you study psychology, but it won't amount to much more than a footnote.

Back to today, we are faced with AI making giant steps towards overtaking most if not all humans combined in any cognitive domain. Chess 1997, Go 2017, Grammar in natural language, 2022, Programming 2025, significant parts of Mathematics, 2026. These giant steps are bounded by 4 things: computer chips, available electricity for your computer chips, training data, as well as smart programmers and mathematicians. The first to are mere logistics, while smart technical people are becoming more and more obsolete. Acquiring training data, however, may pose a few more problems in the future. Training data can be split into two parts: (a) sensory-like data carrying no specific signal about task supervision and (b) supervisory signals.
